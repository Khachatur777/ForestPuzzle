Forest Puzzle — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 October 2026

Forest Puzzle ("the game") is made by Khachatur Petrosyan ("we"). This policy explains what information the game uses and why.

What the game keeps on your device

The game saves your progress on your own device: the levels you have played, your stars, coins and boosters, the animals in your album, your house and your settings (language, sound, music). This information never leaves your device, and we cannot see it. Deleting the game deletes it.

The game has no accounts and does not ask for your name, e-mail address, contacts, photos, location or any other personal information.

Ads

The game is free and shows ads from Google AdMob. To show ads and count them, Google AdMob may collect information from your device, such as:

  • the device's advertising identifier (on iPhone and iPad only if you allow tracking when the game asks);
  • the IP address, the device model, the operating system and its language;
  • which ads were shown and tapped.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the game asks for your consent before ads may be personalised. On iPhone and iPad the game also asks whether ads may use your device's advertising identifier. If you say no, you still see ads, but they are not based on your interests. You can change your choice at any time in your device's settings (on iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking).

Google's use of this information is described in Google's privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy and in "How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services": https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

Purchases

You can buy "Remove ads" inside the game. The purchase is handled entirely by the App Store (or Google Play). We never see your payment details; the game only learns whether the purchase was made, so that it can stop showing ads between levels.

Checking for updates

When it starts, the game reads a small file on its website (hosted by GitHub Pages) to learn whether a newer version is out, and asks you to update when your version is too old to play. The game sends nothing about you with this request. Like any visit to a website, it shows your IP address to GitHub; see GitHub's privacy statement: https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement

Children

The game is meant for a general audience and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.
